Based on the convergence of ERCOT grid data, utility Integrated Resource Plans (IRPs), and hyperscaler campus announcements, the Texas utilities most likely to issue significant engineering contracts over the next 18 months for AI-driven load growth are **Oncor Electric Delivery**, **CenterPoint Energy**, **LCRA (Lower Colorado River Authority)**, and **Austin Energy**.

Because Texas has a deregulated power market, the "utilities" issuing these contracts are primarily Transmission and Distribution (T&D) providers, as the data centers themselves procure generation separately.

Here is a breakdown of the specific utilities, the evidence supporting the conclusion, and the types of engineering contracts they will issue.

---

### 1. Oncor Electric Delivery (Dallas-Fort Worth & West Texas)
Oncor is the undisputed epicenter of AI load growth in Texas. The DFW metroplex—and specifically the "Data Center Alley" corridor stretching from Richardson to McKinney, and now exploding south into Ellis and Navarro counties—represents the densest concentration of data centers in the state.

*   **The Evidence:**
    *   **Oracle’s 1.2 GW Campus:** Oracle is building a massive, multi-building AI data center campus in Ellis County. This requires Oncor to build entirely new, high-capacity transmission infrastructure to serve a load equivalent to a small city.
    *   **Oncor’s Regulatory Filings:** In recent filings with the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT), Oncor has explicitly cited unprecedented load growth from data centers. They have noted that traditional load forecasting models are underestimating growth, prompting emergency and accelerated capital expenditure (CapEx) approvals for grid hardening and expansion.
    *   **ERCOT Queue Data:** The ERCOT Grid Interconnection Queue (GRID) shows an overwhelming concentration of active requests in Oncor’s service territory, heavily weighted toward behind-the-meter (BTM) data center loads paired with solar and battery storage.
*   **Likely Engineering Contracts:** Substation design (345kV to 138kV step-downs), transmission line routing and surveying for new greenfield corridors in Ellis/Navarro counties, and distribution feeder upgrades.

### 2. CenterPoint Energy (Houston Metro)
Historically reliant on petrochemical and industrial load, the Houston area is experiencing a sudden, massive influx of data center development because DFW is running out of immediately available power blocks and developable land.

*   **The Evidence:**
    *   **The "Katy Corridor" Boom:** Submarkets like Katy, Fort Bend County, and Waller County are seeing explosive leasing by AI hyperscalers. Major developers like QTS, DataBank, and CyrusOne are aggressively building here.
    *   **Load Growth Revisions:** In late 2023 and through 2024, CenterPoint revised its load growth forecasts significantly upward, specifically naming data center migration from DFW to Houston as a primary driver.
    *   **CapEx Plans:** CenterPoint’s recent earnings calls and rate case filings indicate a planned increase in T&D CapEx, with a specific focus on "economic development" projects, which is utility terminology for securing large industrial/data center loads.
*   **Likely Engineering Contracts:** Upgrades to existing 138kV substations in the Katy/Fort Bend area, routing studies for new 138kV/345kV transmission loops to serve the western Houston suburbs, and civil/geotechnical engineering for substation expansion.

### 3. LCRA (Lower Colorado River Authority) (Central Texas)
LCRA is a wholesale generator and transmission provider serving dozens of co-ops and municipalities. Their territory includes the "Texas Silicon Hills" (Austin, Round Rock, Georgetown) and the rapidly growing data center hub in Hutto and Taylor.

*   **The Evidence:**
    *   **Meta’s Temple Campus:** Meta is building a 1+ GW data center in Temple (partially served by LCRA transmission infrastructure). This requires massive regional grid reinforcement.
    *   **The Hutto/Taylor Explosion:** NTT, Switch, and others have massive campuses in this corridor. Because these towns were historically small agricultural communities, the existing T&D infrastructure is entirely insufficient for gigawatt-scale AI loads.
    *   **LCRA’s Transmission Plan:** LCRA has publicly stated it is fast-tracking transmission projects in Williamson and Bell counties to accommodate tech sector growth, bypassing traditional multi-year planning cycles.
*   **Likely Engineering Contracts:** Environmental and routing surveys for new high-voltage transmission lines connecting the wind/solar-rich west Texas areas to the data center-heavy central Texas load centers, and complex interconnection engineering for massive BTM generation sites.

### 4. Austin Energy (City of Austin)
While Austin has strict water and zoning codes that have pushed some hyperscalers to outlying areas (like Hutto/Taylor), the city itself still hosts major AI players (e.g., Google, Oracle) and is facing acute grid constraints.

*   **The Evidence:**
    *   **Austin Energy’s Resource Plan:** In its 2024 Generation and Resource Planning update, Austin Energy identified data centers as the primary threat to its ability to maintain resource adequacy, noting that localized grid congestion in East/Northeast Austin is already severe.
    *   **Moratoriums and Zoning:** The city council has debated moratoriums on new data center connections in certain grid-constrained pockets because the utility literally cannot engineer and build the distribution infrastructure fast enough.
*   **Likely Engineering Contracts:** Distributed Energy Resource (DER) engineering (designing systems where data centers use their own batteries to island during peak times), advanced SCADA and grid control engineering, and targeted 138kV substation upgrades in Northeast Austin.

### The Macro Evidence: Why the Next 18 Months?
Engineering contracts precede construction by 12 to 24 months. The rush to issue contracts *now* is driven by three macro-level data points:

1.  **ERCOT's Revised Load Forecast:** In May 2024, ERCOT released an updated Long-Term System Assessment showing peak demand could double by 2030, reaching up to 152 GW. ERCOT CEO Pablo Vegas explicitly identified AI data centers as the primary variable causing forecasters to revise their models upward.
2.  **The "Behind-the-Meter" Complexity:** AI data centers in Texas rarely connect to the grid normally. They connect via "BTM" arrangements where they build gigawatts of solar, wind, and batteries directly adjacent to the servers, and use the utility grid only as backup. Engineering a substation that can safely switch between islanded BTM power and grid power during a grid emergency requires highly specialized, custom engineering studies that must happen before a single wire is strung.
3.  **Construction Lead Times:** Even if a data center developer breaks ground today, the utility interconnection process—requiring dozens of engineering studies (System Impact Studies, Facilities Studies)—takes 18 to 36 months. Utilities *must* issue these engineering contracts in the next 18 months if they hope to have the physical infrastructure ready for the AI campuses slated to come online in 2026 and 2027.

### What to Look For (Actionable Intelligence)
If you are tracking these opportunities, monitor the **PUCT Project Tracking System**. Look for utilities filing "Certificate of Convenience and Necessity" (CCN) amendments or new routing applications in **Ellis, Navarro, Williamson, Bell, and Fort Bend counties**. These regulatory filings are the legally required precursors to issuing the actual engineering and design (E&D) contracts.
