Unlike Texas, Florida’s electricity market is **vertically integrated and traditionally regulated**. This is a critical distinction: when an AI data center requires power in Florida, the local utility must supply *both* the wires (T&D) *and* the generation (power plants).

Because of this structural difference, the engineering contracts issued in Florida over the next 18 months will not just be for grid interconnection, but heavily weighted toward **generation engineering, power plant upgrades, and massive transmission corridors**.

Based on 2024 Florida Public Service Commission (FPSC) filings, subsea cable landing advantages, and hyperscaler announcements, the utilities most likely to issue AI-driven engineering contracts are **Florida Power & Light (FPL)**, **Duke Energy Florida**, and **JEA (Jacksonville)**.

Here is the breakdown of the utilities, the evidence, and the specific engineering contracts expected.

---

### 1. Florida Power & Light (FPL)
FPL is the undisputed heavyweight in this space. It serves the two hottest data center markets in Florida: South Florida (Miami/Dade/Broward) for edge computing and financial AI, and Central Florida (Osceola/Orange counties) for massive hyperscale campuses.

*   **The Evidence:**
    *   **2024 Ten-Year Site Plan:** In their May 2024 filing, FPL officially abandoned decades of flat load-growth projections. They forecast a staggering **85% increase in baseload demand over the next decade**, explicitly naming data centers and AI as the primary drivers alongside population growth.
    *   **Generation Squeeze:** FPL has admitted that their current fleet of solar-plus-battery and existing gas plants cannot support the 24/7 baseload profile of AI data centers.
    *   **Specific Campuses:** While Meta paused a traditional data center in Flagler County, AI hyperscalers (like Microsoft and QTS) are actively expanding in Osceola County. Furthermore, specialized AI firms (like CoreWeave) are actively scouting South Florida for latency-sensitive financial/trading AI applications.
    *   **Nuclear Expansion:** FPL is fast-tracking the **Turkey Point 6 & 7 nuclear expansion**. While nuclear takes a decade, the early-site permitting, environmental engineering, and initial civil engineering contracts will be issued in this 18-month window, largely justified by the need to firm up AI/data center baseload.
*   **Likely Engineering Contracts:**
    *   *Generation:* Front-End Engineering Design (FEED) for new combined-cycle natural gas plants and early-site nuclear engineering for Turkey Point.
    *   *T&D:* 230kV/500kV transmission routing and substation design in Osceola County to support the "Siloam" and "Mesa" substation areas which are reaching capacity.

### 2. Duke Energy Florida
Duke Energy Florida serves the I-4 corridor (Tampa to Orlando), which is experiencing a massive surge in data center leasing as companies seek alternatives to the constrained power markets in Northern Virginia and California.

*   **The Evidence:**
    *   **2024 Integrated Resource Plan (IRP):** Duke Energy Florida’s recent IRP highlighted "unprecedented large-load growth." They noted a massive spike in requests for proposals (RFPs) from data center developers, forcing them to revise their load forecasts upward by several gigawatts over the next 5 years.
    *   **The I-4 Corridor Boom:** Polk County and southern Orange County are seeing a boom in industrial zoning approvals for data centers. Companies like Equinix (via the acquisition of Switch/SUPERNAP) and Digital Realty are expanding footprints here.
    *   **Capacity Procurement:** Because Duke FL does not have enough immediate generation, they have recently issued RFPs for *purchasing* power from third-party developers to cover the interim gap while they engineer and build their own new plants.
*   **Likely Engineering Contracts:**
    *   *Generation:* FEED contracts for advanced combustion turbine (simple and combined cycle) plants to be built in the 2027-2029 timeframe, but engineered starting *now*.
    *   *T&D:* Upgrading the 230kV backbone in Polk and Hillsborough counties, and engineering advanced distribution systems to handle "clustered" data center loads.

### 3. JEA (Jacksonville Electric Authority)
JEA is a municipal utility, but Jacksonville has emerged as a stealth mega-hub for AI infrastructure due to a unique geographic advantage: it is the primary landing point for subsea fiber-optic cables connecting the US to South America and Europe. AI requires low-latency global data transfer.

*   **The Evidence:**
    *   **Subsea Cable Landings:** Jacksonville hosts major cable landing stations (e.g., the AMX-1, BRUSA, and others). Hyperscalers building AI clusters for global inference want to be as close to these landing points as physically possible.
    *   **Large Load Shifts:** JEA’s recent load forecasts show a distinct shift from traditional heavy industry to digital infrastructure. They have publicly stated they are actively working with "several large technology customers" regarding multi-hundred-megawatt loads.
    *   **The Northside Generating Station:** JEA is currently executing a massive engineering and construction overhaul of their Northside plant, converting older units to modern, efficient natural gas. While started before the AI boom, the timeline aligns perfectly with securing baseload for incoming digital loads.
*   **Likely Engineering Contracts:**
    *   *T&D:* Highly specialized substation engineering near the Jacksonville port and beach areas to route massive power to cable-landing-adjacent parcels.
    *   *Grid Modernization:* Engineering for advanced Volt/VAR optimization and battery storage integration to handle the rapid, localized spikes in power demand from AI inference tasks.

### The Macro Evidence: Why Florida is Different (and Urgent)

1.  **The "Baseload" Crisis:** In Texas, data centers bring their own solar and batteries. In Florida, data centers generally expect the utility to provide firm, 24/7 power. Florida utilities are suddenly realizing they do not have enough *nighttime* power (when solar drops off) to run AI servers. This is triggering an emergency scramble for gas and nuclear engineering contracts.
2.  **Water Constraints:** Florida is facing severe water aquifer issues. Traditional data centers use massive amounts of water for cooling. Engineering contracts issued in the next 18 months will heavily feature **mechanical engineering for alternative cooling** (liquid cooling, direct-to-chip, or hybrid adiabatic systems) because Florida utilities and water management districts are restricting water usage.
3.  **The FPSC "Large User" Dockets:** If you monitor the Florida Public Service Commission docket system, you will see a spike in "Determination of Need" (DON) applications over the next 6-12 months. Before a utility can build a power plant or major transmission line for these AI loads, they must get a DON from the FPSC. The engineering studies submitted as exhibits in these dockets represent millions of dollars in active engineering contracts being issued *right now*.

### Summary of What to Watch For
Unlike Texas (where you watch the ERCOT queue), in Florida, you watch the **FPSC Ten-Year Site Plans** and **Determination of Need (DON) filings**. Look for FPL and Duke to file DONs for new 1,500+ MW combined-cycle gas plants in Central/South Florida in late 2024 or early 2025. The engineering firms that win the FEED contracts for those plants will be the primary beneficiaries of the AI load boom in Florida over the next 18 months.
