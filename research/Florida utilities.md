# Florida Utilities and AI-Driven Load Growth: Engineering Contract Outlook (Next 18 Months)

## Executive Summary

This report analyzes the likelihood of major Florida utilities issuing engineering contracts over the next 18 months, driven by significant AI-driven load growth and the expansion of data centers. Based on recent regulatory filings, public statements, and news reports, **Florida Power & Light (FPL)** and **Duke Energy Florida (DEF)** are highly likely to issue substantial engineering contracts. These utilities are proactively investing in new generation capacity, transmission infrastructure, and grid modernization to accommodate anticipated demand from large-scale data centers. While **JEA (Jacksonville Electric Authority)** also acknowledges load growth, specific, immediate engineering contract signals related to AI/data centers are less explicit in publicly available documents compared to FPL and DEF.

## Introduction

The rapid proliferation of Artificial Intelligence (AI) technologies and the corresponding demand for data processing have led to an unprecedented surge in electricity consumption by data centers. This phenomenon is prompting electric utilities across the United States to revise their load growth forecasts and accelerate infrastructure development. Florida, with its growing economy and strategic location, is emerging as a target for data center development. This report investigates which Florida utilities are best positioned to issue engineering contracts within the next 18 months to address this AI-driven load growth, supported by evidence from their Integrated Resource Plans (IRPs), Ten-Year Site Plans (TYSPs), and other public disclosures.

## Key Utilities and Evidence of AI-Driven Load Growth

### Florida Power & Light (FPL)

FPL, the largest electric utility in Florida, is actively preparing for a significant increase in demand from data centers. The utility anticipates adding 335,000 new customers by the end of the decade, a projection that includes substantial load from large-scale data centers [1]. To manage this growth responsibly and protect existing ratepayers, FPL has implemented a
new rate structure that ensures large load customers, including data centers, fund 100% of the cost of new power generation and associated infrastructure required to serve them [1] [2].

FPL's 2026-2035 Ten-Year Power Plant Site Plan outlines significant investments in new generation and battery storage. While the full document is extensive, snippets indicate plans for substantial solar generation and battery storage capacity additions [3]. These additions are crucial for meeting the increased demand from data centers, which can consume as much electricity as a medium-sized city [4]. The requirement for data centers to fund engineering studies to evaluate project scope, feasibility, and grid connection costs directly signals upcoming engineering contracts [1]. FPL expects to announce its first large-load data center deals in 2026 [5].

### Duke Energy Florida (DEF)

Duke Energy Florida is also proactively addressing the anticipated influx of data centers and AI-driven load growth. DEF filed a proposal in September 2025 to the Florida Public Service Commission (PSC) to establish a framework for serving data centers, aiming to balance economic development with protecting existing customers [6]. This proposal includes creating a new customer class for large load customers and establishing corresponding rate schedules and uniform policies [6].

Duke Energy's 2025 Ten-Year Site Plan, submitted in April 2025, acknowledges the expansion of data centers and AI infrastructure as a key driver for future resource needs [7]. The company has secured approximately 7.6 GW of data center electric service agreements, with 2.7 GW signed since February 2026 [8]. This significant contracting activity for power supply directly implies a need for substantial engineering work related to new generation, transmission upgrades, and substation development. Duke Energy's capital expenditure plan includes funding for 14 GW of generation and 4.5 GW of batteries for the AI data center surge [9]. Specific transmission projects, such as the Almond Transmission Line (siting study and route selection July 2025 - March 2026, preconstruction March 2026 - 2027) and upgrades to the Ginnie to Haile Tap to Archer Transmission Line (starting 2025, with January 2026 milestones), indicate ongoing and future engineering contracts [10] [11]. Additionally, the Turfway Reliability Project involves a new substation and 138-kV transmission lines, with surveying and right-of-way acquisition planned for 2025-2026 [12].

### JEA (Jacksonville Electric Authority)

JEA, serving the Jacksonville area, also recognizes the growing demand from data centers. Their 2026 Ten-Year Site Plan is available through the Florida PSC website [13]. While the specific details regarding AI-driven load growth and dedicated data center infrastructure projects require a deeper dive into the full document, JEA, like other Florida utilities, is subject to the broader trends of increased electricity demand from data centers [14]. The presence of a 2026 Ten-Year Site Plan suggests ongoing planning for future load, which would inherently involve engineering considerations for system expansion and upgrades.

## Evidence Supporting Engineering Contracts

The primary evidence supporting the likelihood of engineering contracts stems from the utilities' proactive planning and regulatory filings:

*   **Rate Case Filings and Tariffs:** FPL and Duke Energy Florida have both established new rate structures and tariffs specifically designed for large load customers, including data centers. These tariffs often require data centers to fund engineering studies and infrastructure upgrades, directly leading to engineering contracts [1] [6].
*   **Ten-Year Site Plans (TYSPs) and Integrated Resource Plans (IRPs):** These annual filings with the Florida Public Service Commission detail projected load growth, planned generation additions, transmission projects, and capital expenditures over a ten-year horizon. The 2026 TYSPs for FPL, Duke Energy Florida, and JEA all reflect planning for future demand, much of which is now explicitly linked to data centers and AI [3] [7] [13].
*   **Announced Projects and Investments:** FPL's plans for new solar and battery storage, and Duke Energy Florida's specific transmission line and substation projects, are concrete examples of infrastructure development that necessitate engineering design, procurement, and construction oversight [3] [10] [11] [12]. Duke Energy's substantial capital plan increase to $103 billion through 2029, partly driven by data center demand, further underscores the scale of anticipated engineering work [9].
*   **Statements from Utility Leadership:** Executives from FPL and Duke Energy have publicly acknowledged the impact of data centers and AI on their load forecasts and the need for significant investments [1] [6] [8].

## Conclusion

Florida Power & Light and Duke Energy Florida are the most likely utilities to issue significant engineering contracts over the next 18 months due to AI-driven load growth. Their comprehensive planning, specific rate structures for large loads, and announced infrastructure projects provide strong evidence of impending engineering work. While JEA is also engaged in long-term planning, more detailed information on their specific AI/data center related engineering contracts would require a deeper analysis of their full 2026 Ten-Year Site Plan. The overall trend across Florida utilities indicates a substantial increase in engineering demand to support the burgeoning AI and data center industry.
